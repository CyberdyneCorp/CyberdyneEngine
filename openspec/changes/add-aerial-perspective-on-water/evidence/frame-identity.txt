THE FIRST OFF TAKE WAS NOT IDENTICAL, AND WHY. water.slang as first written kept one body and
branched on the table's `enabled` word as it went (`if (airOn && bentDepth > 0.0)`, and an early
`if (airOn) return` before composing `lerp`, `+ glitter`, `lerp` for off). With the table off, 14 of
192 frames (4, 9, 12, 41, 53, 68, 69, 72, 80, 113, 125, 167, 171, 180) differed from before-off in
exactly one sea pixel each, by one 8-bit step. The sample is deterministic (above), so this was the
shader. Moving the off composition back to where the old shader did it, with the runtime branch
kept, still gave 14 of 192. Two copies of the body chosen once in the entry point —
`shadeWater<true>` and `shadeWater<false>`, the off copy the old body operation for operation —
give 0 of 192, and so did those two copies with the flag read at run time inside each; so the
difference is the driver's code generation for the one-body shape, not the arithmetic. The
committed shader is the two-copy one.

render.world_water_aerial_perspective does not see this: its level sea has no waves, cloud
shadows or foam, and its off case stayed at 0 differing texels under the one-body shader, and under
it again with the sample's shoreline foam and caustics switched on in the suite's scene. The guard
for the sample's frame is this comparison, as it is for `add-aerial-perspective`.
