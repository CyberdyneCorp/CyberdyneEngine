# Design: M11.c — Image

## 1. The spike, and what it has to answer before anything below is scoped

**M11.c's named risk is that the path this whole rung stands on has never been run.** Not a
component of it — every component is built, tested and gated — but the *path*: a material authored in
the editor, lowered by the runtime compiler, its textures encoded and cooked, bound through the
bindless table the shader standard library already declares, drawn in a frame assembled out of the
engine's own passes, and photographed. Each stage exists. No target in the tree runs two of them in
sequence with content in between.

The spike is `~/cyberdyne-spikes/m11c-material-spike/`, outside the repository as M3's through M10's
were, because a prototype under `docs/` fails `just quality-layers`, correctly. It is time-boxed, it
is not merged, and its only deliverable is a decision recorded here.

### 1.1 The question, stated so that it can come back "no"

> Authored in the editor, compiled, encoded, bound, assembled, captured — does one textured material
> survive all six, and what does each junction cost?

It is deliberately one material and not a scene. A scene would measure art direction, which is a
judgement; one material measures whether the junctions exist, which is a fact. The junctions are
where this project's renderer history says the cost is: M8.b assembled a frame and stopped one layer
short of anything recording into it, and M8.c had to build `src/rendering/pipeline/` before a
particle could be drawn.

### 1.2 What the spike refuses to report

Two preconditions run before any picture is kept, and both must be watched to fail, because this
project has shipped a determinism test that passed on the defect it was written for and a
`materials_seen` assertion that asked only for `> 1`:

- **A material whose textures are unbound must produce a visibly different picture**, and the spike
  exits non-zero if it does not. A bindless index that resolves to a default and shades plausibly is
  the single most likely way for this rung to photograph a lie.
- **A frame with the post chain removed must produce a visibly different picture.** If tone mapping
  off changes nothing, the frame is not going through the chain, and every later claim about
  exposure and anti-aliasing is a claim about a code path the picture never took.

### 1.3 What it cannot answer on this host

Through the ledger's `requires`/`where` mechanism rather than as a sentence, the way
`m10:pcg-gpu-domain-agreement` does it. This machine has **one GPU vendor**, so an image judged here
is an image judged against one driver's rasterisation and one vendor's floating point; and
`cy::rhi::Capability::RayTracing` is an enumerator **nothing sets** in
`src/backends/rhi/vulkan/src/vulkan_instance.cpp`, so on this tree every ray-traced tier runs its
software fallback and a spike cannot measure the hardware one by wanting to.

### 1.4 The honest asymmetry, named rather than dressed up

**This spike measures the artefact's feasibility. It does not measure the row this design predicts it
will demote.** M10's design predicted `procedural-content-generation` and was wrong in the useful
direction *because a spike measured it* — twenty-four configurations over twelve trials — and that is
the standard this document is held to. It is not met here: §4's demotion prediction is a judgement
about device work on a one-vendor host, not a measurement. If this rung wants that prediction
measured rather than asserted, the cheapest honest way is a second, much smaller probe at the head of
section 4 — a hierarchical depth buffer built against the `Hzb` and `TwoPassCull` CPU model already
in the tree, compared for agreement — and it should be run before `rendering-culling-and-lod`'s
Complete cell is defended, not after.

## 2. Why the compilers are first, and why the editor is finished before this rung

**`material-compiler` and `shader-system` are section 1 of the tasks because image quality is
expressed through them.** A Complete `rendering-global-illumination` standing on a material compiler
that is not Complete is a claim about the picture resting on the thing that draws it; the ordering is
not aesthetic.

Both rows have exactly one open requirement and it is one absence read from two sides: *"Node
previews use the real compiler"* has no graph to preview, and *"Visual material editor"* has no
panel. The panel is **M11.b's**. What this rung owes is the half that makes the panel honest — the
preview is generated by the same compiler `cy_material compile` drives, and every lowering stage the
command line prints is reachable through the panel and agrees with it on the same graph.

**And the editor is finished before this rung deliberately.** An art-directed shot assembled by hand
in C++ proves the renderer and nothing else. One authored *through* the editor proves the renderer
and the editor at once, and is the only honest demonstration that this is a usable engine rather than
a set of passing suites. That ordering is the reason M11.b sits above M11.c in the split, and it is
also the reason §4's first contingency is about M11.b rather than about anything in this rung.

## 3. Three seams, and the one that is two rows counted once

### 3.1 The assembled frame

`samples/10-world/CMakeLists.txt` links `cy::environment`, `cy::foliage`, `cy::pcg`,
`cy::rendering-graph`, `cy::rendering-sky`, `cy::rhi`, `cy::terrain`, `cy::water`, `cy::weather` and
`cy::world`. It does **not** link `cy::rendering-assembly`, `cy::rendering-post`,
`cy::rendering-temporal`, `cy::rendering-forward` or `cy::rendering-shadows`. Outside
`src/rendering/`, exactly two targets link `cy::rendering-assembly` — which is what pulls
`cy::rendering-post`, `cy::rendering-shadows`, `cy::rendering-sky`, `cy::rendering-temporal` and
`cy::rendering-forward` in — and they are `samples/08-vertical-slice` and one `cy::vfx` test binary.
**The largest picture this project has published never passed through tone mapping or an
anti-aliasing stage**, which is why most of tasks section 3 is one edit followed by tuning rather
than new architecture.

And the two published sample lineages are **disjoint**. `samples/07-fidelity` links
`cy::rendering-virtual-geometry`, `cy::rendering-gi` (which pulls denoise and ray tracing) and
`cy::rendering-arbiter`, and links no assembly, no post and no temporal; `samples/08-vertical-slice`
links the assembly and none of the first three. **No target in the tree links both
`cy::rendering-gi` and `cy::rendering-assembly`** — so global illumination has never appeared in an
assembled frame, and the beauty shot is the first frame that would have to contain both.

### 3.2 The GI/atmosphere seam, which the plan has promised twice

[Dependency cycle 2](../../../docs/roadmap/dependencies.md) is the whole reason
`rendering-global-illumination`'s Complete cell was at M10: an analytic sky seeds at M7, the physical
atmosphere lands at M10, *"and GI reaches Complete there"*. The atmosphere landed; the seam was never
joined. `src/rendering/gi/` links `cy::rendering-denoise` and `cy::rendering-raytracing` and not
`cy::rendering-sky`; `sky_light.h` names `gi::SkyTerm` only in comments describing the shape it
mirrors; nothing outside a test constructs one.

`dependencies.md` calls joining it *"one adapter at one composition point"*. **This design does not
assume that is true.** One requirement of twenty-nine is the whole difference, which makes it the
rung's cheapest Complete on paper — and the requirement's second half is that sky-derived
illumination updates *incrementally where the sky changes continuously*, *under the illumination
budget*, invalidating *only* the illumination that depends on it. An adapter constructs a term; it
does not decide what a moving sun invalidates in a radiance cache. If the seam turns out to be that
design question rather than an adapter, the task list says to write down what it actually was.

### 3.3 Two pieces of device work that five rows share

This is the part of the rung most likely to be double-counted, so it is stated once:

| Device work | Rows that are waiting on exactly it |
|---|---|
| **A hierarchical depth buffer on the device** | `rendering-culling-and-lod` — `GpuCullPass::upload` refuses `kGpuCullOcclusion` (`cull_pass.cpp:380`) naming the absent HZB. `virtual-geometry` — cluster-granular occlusion, whose seam is already cut: the shader takes the cull tests in the fixed order and `TraversalStatistics::nodes_pruned_by_occlusion` reads zero |
| **An RHI ray-tracing capability** | `ray-tracing-infrastructure` — the service runs on the CPU because nothing sets `Capability::RayTracing`. `virtual-shadows` — `ShadowMode::RayTraced` and `Hybrid` are named by `address_space.h` and are *"`ray-tracing-infrastructure`'s to supply"*. `rendering-global-illumination` — its hardware tracing tier is the same service |

**One piece of work must not be counted as two Complete cells.** The ledger's own
`criteria.fingerprint` collapses declarations that run the same command, which is the mechanism that
makes this visible rather than a matter of discipline: if two rows' criteria are the same command,
the ledger reports one check and the rung would be reading two.

The second row of that table also carries this rung's clearest boundary problem. Setting
`Capability::RayTracing` in the Vulkan backend is a backend edit this rung can make; making the claim
*portable* is `rhi-and-render-graph`, which is **M11.d's row**. A Complete
`ray-tracing-infrastructure` measured on one backend on one vendor is a narrow claim and the
criterion has to say so in its own words.

## 4. Which rows are actually contingent, stated rather than assumed

Fifteen rows to Complete is a large load, and the honest position at scoping time is that they are
not equally risky. In descending order of how likely this rung is to have to demote them:

- **`rendering-culling-and-lod` is the row this rung predicts it may demote.** A hierarchical depth
  buffer and cluster-granular occlusion are device work and this host has one GPU vendor. A
  device-free criterion can check the model — `Hzb` and `TwoPassCull` are a CPU model and are tested
  as one — and only a device can check the pass. A row whose last requirement can be judged only on
  hardware nobody else has is a row whose claim has to be narrow, and §1.4 says plainly that this
  prediction is a judgement and not a measurement.
- **`ray-tracing-infrastructure` is the second candidate, for a different reason**: not that the work
  is hard but that the *claim* is. Its device half is, in its own README's words, "two edits and no
  interface change", and both edits land in a backend whose row belongs to another rung. This one
  demotes if the honest reading is "Vulkan only".
- **`vfx-system` is the third, and its blocker is above it rather than below.** Four absences are
  recorded by name in `src/vfx/README.md` — the six renderer kinds that publish rows nothing
  composites, collision response, an event chain one level deep, and the VFX graph editor. The first
  three are renderer work and belong here. **The fourth is M11.b's editor surface**, and if M11.b did
  not build it the row does not complete in this rung no matter what the renderer does.
- **`atmosphere-sky-and-clouds` is not a prediction; it is a gate.** `status.yaml` already states the
  condition: *the row does NOT reach Complete at M11 while `m10:sky-field-round-trip` is open*. That
  gap is **M11.a's** to close. This rung owns the consumer half — illumination reading the cloud
  shadow field — and a consumer written against a field that returns its declared default at every
  point is a consumer that cannot be tested. If M11.a does not close it, this row stays at Working
  and this rung says so rather than arguing around thirteen requirements with one open.
- **`rendering-global-illumination` is the row with the longest history of being promised** — M7,
  then M10 by cycle 2, now here. One requirement of twenty-nine is the whole difference. It is the
  rung's cheapest Complete on paper and the one most likely to reveal that the remaining difference
  is a design question; §3.2.
- **`material-compiler`, `shader-system`, `rendering-post-processing`, `temporal-rendering`,
  `rendering-lighting-and-shadows`, `rendering-architecture` and `rendering-geometry-and-resources`
  are the well-understood half.** Each has a single named open requirement against a mechanism that
  is built and measured, and none depends on a question this design cannot answer. They are not free
  — `rendering-geometry-and-resources` needs a pose representation that `animation-and-skinning`
  owns, and `rendering-architecture` needs a real cost measurement rather than another declaration —
  but they are work, not unknowns.

**And the demotion this design would rather predict than discover.** If the spike's answer is that
the authoring path does not close — no encoder, no panel, or a junction that does not join — then
this rung's whole artefact degrades to another debug view, and **the correct outcome is to publish
that and demote the image rows rather than to photograph a hand-assembled C++ frame and present it as
art direction.** A hand-assembled shot would be a *better picture* and a *worse claim*, and this
ladder has consistently chosen the claim.

## 6. What this rung deliberately does not do

- **No new renderer architecture.** The render graph, the frame assembly, the pipeline layer, the
  material compiler and the shader system are M3's, M7's, M8.b's and M8.c's settled answers. This
  rung uses them from the world and the game rather than beside them.
- **No second material model.** `cy/material.slang` is a Slang interface with one method returning a
  `Surface`, chosen so the light loop and pass structure stay the engine's. Real content is the test
  of that choice, not an occasion to revisit it.
- **No golden-image test of the beauty shot.** A still drawn on a device, with wall-clock-tuned
  exposure, is exactly the shape M10 refused to byte-compare: a tolerance wide enough to survive a
  second GPU would not detect a changed picture either. What is checked instead is the *provenance* —
  the stage list the frame ran, which inputs were textures, and whether the negative controls still
  change the image.
- **No art direction as a criterion.** "Does it look good" is a judgement and stays one. What the
  ledger checks is that the passes ran, the materials were textures, the frame was captured pinned
  and the caption matches the manifest.

## 7. The artefact's honesty contract

The beauty shot is the only artefact on this ladder whose quality is a judgement rather than a
measurement, and that is exactly why it carries obligations the others do not:

1. **It is captured, not drawn**, through the same assembled frame the engine ships, from a committed
   scene and a committed material set, by a recipe — the way `just capture-world` produces its still
   from nothing but a seed.
2. **It publishes its provenance beside it**: resolution, exposure, which passes ran, what is
   authored content and what the renderer produced, what was hand-placed and what was procedural, and
   what the frame cost.
3. **It says what it is not.** Three authored materials and the rest constants is a caption that says
   three. A normals debug view stays labelled a normals debug view — `docs/design/virtual-geometry.md`
   captions its frame *"Resolved world normals under one light"* and its level-of-detail captures
   *"one colour per cluster"*, and those captions are correct today and stay correct.
4. **It ships with its before/after**, the same frame with the post chain and with none, because that
   difference is most of tasks sections 1 and 3 and no number shows it.

**A beautiful picture with an unstated provenance is the most efficient way to make this whole record
dishonest.** Every other artefact on this ladder could be checked by running it again; this one is
checked by believing the caption, so the caption is the deliverable and the image is the evidence for
it.
