# Add selection outlines and unit highlights to the frame

## Why

`editor-viewport-and-gizmos` puts "selection outlines, highlight rendering, and overlays" on the
engine's side of its responsibility split, and `editor-visual-language`'s "Selection appearance"
fixes what a selection looks like: a thin, high-contrast outline that stays legible on bright and
dark content without changing width or brightness, that does not bloom or glow, that does not
obscure the material it surrounds, and a gameplay selection distinguishable from the editor's. The
tree had the appearance rules as data the Rust editor validates, and nothing that drew an outline:
no mask, no edge pass, and no way for a game to say which units are selected.

A strategy game needs more than the editor's case: many units selected in team colours, a softer
highlight for the unit under the cursor, and the parts of a selected unit that a building hides
still shown — dimmed and dashed — so the player does not lose it. No requirement in the tree asks
for that, so this change adds one to `rendering-post-processing`, where the chain's order is.

## What changes

- A new module, `src/rendering/selection/`:
  - `highlight.h`: `HighlightSet` — marks keyed by the draw's stable identity (the entity's bits),
    a kind (selected, hovered) and a display-referred colour, sharing a palette of up to 255
    styles — `OutlineSettings` (widths, glow strength, occluded alpha, dash, fill, depth tolerance),
    and `outline_reference`, the edge pass transcribed for the suites.
  - `outline_pass.h`: `OutlinePass`, two graph passes at the frame's new `SelectionOutlines` stage:
    the marked opaque draws drawn again, through the frame's own geometry, draw records and sets 0
    and 1, into a transient `R32Uint` mask and depth; and a full-screen composite over the
    tonemapped colour that outlines each marked silhouette, tints and dashes what the prepass depth
    says is hidden, and blends premultiplied. Committed SPIR-V and MSL, regenerated by
    `shaders/regenerate.py`.
  - `selection_component.h`: the gameplay face — a `SelectionHighlight` component registered by
    name, and `gather_highlights`, which turns the world's components into the set the pass draws.
- `ForwardFrame` gains the `SelectionOutlines` stage (`FrameFeatures::selection_outlines`), after the
  post-process and before the interface, declared by its producer through `FrameStageDeclaration`;
  `ScreenSpaceStageInputs` carries the frame's draw records. It refuses the feature without a
  producer or without the depth prepass. `FrameAssembly` passes it through
  (`AssemblyDescription::selection_outlines`, `FrameSinks::selection_outlines`).
- Swift: `CyberdyneKit.SelectionHighlight` is the component's layout, and `World.highlight` /
  `World.clearHighlight` add and remove it through the existing ABI entries. No ABI entry is added.
- `pipeline_test::FrameScene` gains a `stable_id` hook, so a caller can key its boxes' draws by
  entity.
- `samples/13-rts-selection`: a squad selected by a drag box, an enemy under the cursor and a unit
  behind a building, marked as components and photographed before and after.
- Tests: `unit.render_forward` (the stage's place and its refusals), `unit.rendering_selection`
  (the marks, the palette, the reference, the embedded module's layout, and the component), `unit.abi_selection` (the component through the C ABI, as Swift reaches it), and
  `render.selection_outlines` (five cases on a Vulkan device), each frame case proved red by a
  mutation.

## Scope

The mask draws the opaque layer — what the prepass drew, so what "hidden" is measured against.
Masked and transparent draws, skinned and virtual geometry, and MSAA-resolved coverage are not
outlined. Two marked objects of the same style that touch share one outline. The composite searches
a disc up to eight pixels wide per pixel; a separable distance transform would cost less at large
widths and is not built. Metal's MSL is generated and committed and not exercised on this host;
D3D12 has no DXIL package.
